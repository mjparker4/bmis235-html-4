Chicken Katsu

Chicken katsu is a boneless chicken cutlet coated in panko breadcrumbs and fried until it's golden and crispy. It gets sliced into strips and served with katsu sauce, which is a thick, sweet, and tangy sauce kind of like a fruity barbecue sauce.

Katsu started in Japan, where the original version, tonkatsu, is made with pork. It came to Hawaii with Japanese immigrants, many of whom first arrived in the late 1800s to work on the sugar plantations. Over time, the chicken version became one of the most popular plate lunches in the islands. You can find it at almost every plate lunch spot, usually with two scoops of rice and mac salad.

Chicken katsu is on my list because it was my go-to order growing up. It's simple, filling, and hard to mess up, so it was always an easy choice after practice or on the way home.

My favorite place to get it on Oahu is L&L. I usually order the chicken katsu plate with extra katsu sauce.
