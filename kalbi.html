Kalbi

Kalbi is Korean-style barbecued beef short ribs. In Hawaii, the ribs are usually cut thin across the bone, so each piece has a few small bones in it. The meat is marinated in soy sauce, sugar, garlic, sesame oil, and green onions, then grilled until it's caramelized on the outside and juicy on the inside.

Kalbi comes from Korea, where it's called galbi. Korean immigrants started arriving in Hawaii in 1903 to work on the sugar plantations, and they brought their food with them. Today, kalbi is a staple at Korean barbecue restaurants and plate lunch spots all over the islands, and it's a favorite at backyard parties and beach days. It's usually served with rice, kimchi, and a few side dishes, or mac salad if you get it as a plate lunch.

Kalbi is on my list because nothing beats the smell of it on the grill. It's sweet, salty, and smoky all at once, and it was always one of the first things to go at any family gathering.

My favorite place to get it on Oahu is L&L. I usually order the kalbi plate.
