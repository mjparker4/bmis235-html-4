Kalua Pork

Kalua pork is one of the most traditional foods in Hawaii. The word "kalua" refers to cooking in an imu, an underground oven. A whole pig is wrapped in ti and banana leaves, then placed in a pit lined with hot lava rocks and covered with dirt. It cooks slowly for hours until the meat is smoky, salty, and tender enough to pull apart by hand.

For a long time, kalua pig was the centerpiece of a luau, and it still is today. Most people don't dig an imu at home anymore, so a lot of families make it in an oven or slow cooker using Hawaiian sea salt and liquid smoke. At plate lunch spots, it usually comes mixed with cabbage, two scoops of rice, and mac salad.

Kalua pork is on my list because it reminds me of family parties back home. Any time there was a graduation, birthday, or big get-together, there was always a tray of kalua pig on the table.

My favorite place to get it on Oahu is L&L. I usually order the kalua pig and cabbage plate.
